One shape, many matrices

Each block is the same box. Only its matrix changes. Drag the picture to look around. The copies stay where their matrices put them.

Look at Draw calls. The number stays at 1. The computer draws every copy in one pass. The copies share one shape. Only their matrices differ.

Color shows height. Color is not in the matrix. Taller blocks are darker.

Where this is used
  • A game or a film draws many trees, blades of grass, or people from one model. A particle effect often draws many copies of one small shape. An environment artist and a technical artist set this up.
  • A city model or a digital twin repeats buildings and street lights. A GIS developer or a digital-twin developer builds that view.
  • An architecture model can repeat every seat in a stadium. A CAD viewer repeats bolts and panels from one part.
  • A science viewer can draw many atoms as copies of one sphere. A scientific visualization developer builds that picture.
  • One draw call keeps the picture steady on a phone, a laptop, or a VR headset. A VR headset needs a high frame rate so the view stays comfortable.
