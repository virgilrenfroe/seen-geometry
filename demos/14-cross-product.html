Cross product

Drag either tip, or set the angle. The red arrow is a × b. It stands perpendicular to both arrows, and its length is the area of the shaded parallelogram. Press Swap a and b and the arrow flips.

Curl the fingers of your right hand from a toward b. Your thumb points the same way as the red arrow. The arrow stands up, out of the page.

The dot product uses cosine and the shadow of one arrow on the other. The cross product uses sine and the area of the parallelogram. In two dimensions, that signed area is the determinant.

    

      
      
    

    

      Where this is used
      
    
        
  • In 3D graphics and in game development, the normal of a polygon is the cross product of two of its edges. A graphics programmer or a game developer uses that normal for lighting, because the brightness depends on how the normal lines up with the light.
    • 
        
  • In mechanical engineering and in physics, torque is r × F. The lever arm r runs from the pivot to the force, and F is the force. A mechanical engineer or a physicist reads the direction of the torque with the right-hand rule.
    • 
        
  • In electrical engineering, the Lorentz force on a moving charge is q (v × B). v is the velocity of the charge and B is the magnetic field. An electrical engineer uses this cross product.
    • 
        
  • In CAD and in GIS, the cross product of two edges of a polygon gives its area, and the sign gives the winding order, clockwise or counterclockwise. A CAD specialist or a GIS analyst uses that sign so every face has a consistent front.
