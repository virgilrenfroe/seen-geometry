Inverse

The inverse of a matrix undoes that matrix, and their product is the identity. If the determinant is 0, there is no inverse.

M is a shear. Slide to 1 to apply M. Slide on to 2 to apply the inverse. The F returns.

      Where this is used
      
    
        
  • A CAD program stores the placement of a part as a matrix. The inverse puts points back into the part's own axes. A mechanical designer uses that after a part has been moved.
    • 
        
  • A game camera uses a matrix to see the world from the camera. The inverse carries a point from the camera back into the world. A graphics programmer sets that up.
    • 
        
  • In a mathematics class, the Hill cipher is a historical example. Letters are encrypted by a matrix and decrypted by its inverse. It is a teaching cipher from 1929, not a modern one.
    • 
      

    

    
    

      Common mistakes
      
    
        
  • Not every matrix has an inverse. A flatten has none, because the determinant is 0 and the lost direction does not come back.
    • 
        
  • The inverse is not the transpose, and it is not the matrix with the sign of every entry flipped.
    • 
        
  • When an inverse exists, the product either way is the identity. The slider on this page applies the matrix first, then the inverse.
    • 
        
  • The Hill cipher decrypts with an inverse. It is a teaching cipher from 1929, not a modern one.
